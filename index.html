Hall settings

Whatever you type shows on the telly, the printouts and the winners poster — and the caller will announce it too. Leave it blank and the hall is simply called “The Bingo Hall”.

Bingo starts at — optional

If you set a time, the idle screen tells everyone when eyes go down — “Bingo tonight at 6:30 pm”.

Hall mode

Each mode changes the whole look of the hall, and — unless you’ve pinned a band below — swaps the band to match. High contrast always wins for readability.

The band

Pin any band you fancy, whatever the hall looks like — Classical brings out the concert grand with Debussy, Beethoven, Mozart, Pachelbel, Brahms, Chopin and Bach. Mix it up shuffles the lot, so it never gets repetitive. Seasonal numbers still slip into the set list.

The residents — one name per line
Deal the boards

“Shuffle & deal” places residents on the boards in play at random — new luck every week. “Deal in order” gives the first resident board 1, and so on. To place someone by hand, tap their name (it turns gold), then tap a board number in the grid. Dashed names are waiting for a board. The roster lives only on this device — never written into the page or sent anywhere.

Match the boards to the sheets you’ve handed out — only the boards in play fill themselves in and win. The quick picks set boards 1 to N; the grid switches individual boards on or off. Changing the boards starts a fresh game. Give a board a name tag and the caller will announce winners by name — “Margaret on board seven!”

Seasonal

Each season dresses the entire hall — background, colours, lights, balls, confetti, the band and the caller. Valentine’s brings floating hearts and Greensleeves; Easter brings falling petals and music-box springtime; the Seaside brings gulls and hornpipes; Halloween, bats; Christmas, snow and carols.

How it all works

  1. Make it yours — set the hall’s name and start time in Hall settings. The name appears on the telly, printouts and posters, and the caller announces it.
  2. Pick your hall — Vintage, Modern or Futuristic — and the band: brass, lounge, synthwave, the classical concert grand, or Mix it up for everything shuffled together. The star button opens Activities: tea break, jukebox, Name That Call, birthday fanfare and tonight’s winners.
  3. Set the boards in play — a quick count, or switch individual boards on and off for exactly the sheets you’ve handed out. Add name tags and the caller announces winners by name.
  4. Dress the hall for the season — Valentine’s, Easter, Seaside Summer, Halloween or Christmas each re-decorate the whole place, with their own music and caller greetings.
  5. Press “Eyes down”. The drum draws 0 to 80, the caller shouts every number with its proper bingo call, announces when a board is one away, and stops after a line until you press “Carry on for the house”. Didn’t catch it? The gold “Say it again” button (or the R key) repeats the last number, nice and slowly.
  6. The full house ends the game — with a photo moment for the winner, names into Tonight’s winners, and posters for the notice board (one game, or the whole evening). The archive remembers every game — and if the page is closed mid-game, it picks up right where it left off.

The caller’s crib sheet — every call, 0 to 80

Accessibility & comfort: three hall modes plus High Contrast; board counts and individual toggles for any group size; name tags so every win is personal; “Say it again” for anyone who misses a call; session recovery so a refresh never loses the game; five bands including the classical library; six seasonal makeovers; a band that dips down while the caller speaks; and caller voice, sound effects, captions and reduced-motion all respected. The boards are fixed into the page — Board 1 is always Board 1, on any device.
